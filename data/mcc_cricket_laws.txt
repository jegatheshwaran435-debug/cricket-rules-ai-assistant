# OFFICIAL LAWS OF CRICKET (MARYLEBONE CRICKET CLUB - MCC) & ICC PLAYING CONDITIONS

================================================================================
LAW 20: DEAD BALL
================================================================================
MCC Law 20.1 - Ball is dead
20.1.1 The ball becomes dead when
20.1.1.1 it is finally settled in the hands of the wicket-keeper or of the bowler.
20.1.1.2 a boundary is scored.
20.1.1.3 a batter is dismissed.
20.1.1.4 whether played or not, it becomes trapped between the bat and person of a batter or between items of his/her clothing or equipment.
20.1.1.5 whether played or not, it lodges in the clothing or equipment of a batter or the clothing of an umpire.
20.1.1.6 under Law 28.3 (Protective helmets not in use), it strikes a helmet placed or resting on the ground.
20.1.1.7 there is an award of penalty runs under either Law 18.5.2 (Deliberate short runs) or Law 42 (Players' conduct).
20.1.1.8 the umpire calls Over or Time.

MCC Law 20.2 - Ball ceases to be dead
The ball ceases to be dead - that is, it comes into play - when the bowler starts his/her run-up or, if there is no run-up, starts his/her bowling action.

MCC Law 20.4 - Umpire calling and signalling Dead ball
20.4.1 Either umpire shall call and signal Dead ball when
20.4.1.1 he/she intervenes in a case of unfair play.
20.4.1.2 a serious injury to a player or umpire occurs.
20.4.1.3 he/she leaves his/her normal position for consulting.
20.4.1.4 one or both bails fall from the striker's wicket before the striker has had the opportunity of playing the ball.
20.4.1.5 the striker is not ready for the delivery of the ball and, if the ball is delivered, makes no attempt to play it.

LAW 21: NO BALL
================================================================================
MCC Law 21.1 - Mode of delivery
21.1.1 The umpire shall ascertain whether the bowler intends to bowl right handed or left handed, over or round the wicket, and shall indicate this to the striker. It is unfair if the bowler fails to notify the umpire of a change in mode of delivery without informing him/her. In this case the umpire shall call and signal No ball.

MCC Law 21.5 - Fair delivery - the feet
For a delivery to be fair in respect of the feet:
21.5.1 The bowler's back foot must land within and not touching the bowling crease or the bowling crease extended.
21.5.2 The bowler's front foot must land with some part of the foot, whether grounded or raised:
21.5.2.1 on the same side of the imaginary line joining the two middle stumps as the return crease from which he/she is bowling, and
21.5.2.2 behind the popping crease. If the front foot lands entirely beyond the popping crease without any part of the shoe grounded or airborne behind the line, the umpire shall call and signal No ball.

MCC Law 21.10 - Ball bouncing more than once, rolling along the ground or pitching off the pitch
The umpire shall call and signal No ball if a ball which he/she considers lawful is delivered and then bounces more than once or rolls along the ground before it reaches the popping crease, or if the ball pitches wholly or partially off the pitch before reaching the line of the striker's wicket.

MCC Law 21.18 - Penalty for a No ball
A penalty of one run shall be awarded to the batting side for a No ball. This penalty shall be in addition to any other runs scored, any boundary allowance, and any penalty runs awarded. A No ball shall not count as one of the 6 balls of the over. Furthermore, in limited overs cricket under ICC Standard Playing Conditions, any No ball results in a Free Hit for the batting side on the next delivery.

LAW 28: THE FIELDER (PROTECTIVE HELMETS & EQUIPMENT)
================================================================================
MCC Law 28.1 - Protective equipment
No fielder other than the wicket-keeper shall be permitted to wear gloves or external leg guards. In addition, protection for the hand or fingers may be worn only with the consent of the umpires.

MCC Law 28.3 - Protective helmets not in use (Ball striking helmet on the ground)
28.3.1 While the ball is in play, if the ball strikes a helmet, or any other piece of protective equipment, not being worn by a fielder and placed or resting on the ground behind the wicket-keeper or elsewhere on the field of play:
28.3.1.1 The ball shall immediately become dead.
28.3.1.2 5 penalty runs shall be awarded to the batting side.
28.3.1.3 The umpire shall award any runs completed by the batters before the ball struck the helmet, together with the run in progress if the batters had crossed at the instant the ball struck the helmet.
28.3.1.4 The ball does not count as one of the over if it was already a No ball or Wide; otherwise the ball shall count as one of the over.
28.3.1.5 The bowler shall not be credited with the runs or charged with the 5 penalty runs; the 5 penalty runs are recorded as extras in the team total.
28.3.1.6 A batter cannot be dismissed out (e.g., caught, run out) after the ball has struck the helmet, because the ball became dead the moment it struck the helmet.

LAW 31: APPEALS
================================================================================
MCC Law 31.1 - Umpire not to give batter out without an appeal
Neither umpire shall give a batter out, even though he/she may be out under the Laws, unless an appeal is made by a fielder of the fielding side.

MCC Law 31.2 - Batters leaving wicket under a misapprehension
An umpire shall intervene if a batter, not having been given out, walks off under a misapprehension that he/she is out. The umpire shall call and signal Dead ball and direct the batter to return.

MCC Law 31.3 - Timing of appeals
For an appeal to be valid, it must be made before the bowler begins his/her run-up or bowling action for the next delivery.

================================================================================
LAW 32: BOWLED
================================================================================
MCC Law 32.1 - Out Bowled
32.1.1 The striker is out Bowled if his/her wicket is broken by a ball delivered by the bowler, not being a No ball, even if the ball first touched the striker's bat or person.
32.1.2 Bowled takes precedence over any other mode of dismissal. For example, if a delivery strikes the striker's pad and would be LBW, but rolls on to break the wicket, the striker is dismissed Bowled, not LBW.

================================================================================
LAW 33: CAUGHT
================================================================================
MCC Law 33.1 - Out Caught
The striker is out Caught if a ball delivered by the bowler, not being a No ball, touches the bat or the hand holding the bat and is subsequently held by a fielder as a fair catch before the ball touches the ground.

MCC Law 33.2 - A fair catch
33.2.1 A catch will only be fair on condition that throughout the act of making the catch:
33.2.1.1 the fielder has no part of his/her person touching the boundary line or the ground beyond the boundary.
33.2.1.2 the ball does not touch the ground at any point before being completely controlled.
33.2.2 The act of making a catch starts from the moment when the fielder first handles the ball and ends when the fielder obtains complete and firm control over both the ball and his/her own movement.

================================================================================
LAW 36: LEG BEFORE WICKET (LBW)
================================================================================
MCC Law 36.1 - Out LBW
The striker is out LBW if all of the following five conditions are met:
36.1.1 The bowler delivers a ball which is not a No ball.
36.1.2 The ball pitches in line between wicket and wicket, or on the off side of the striker's wicket. Note: If the ball pitches outside the leg stump, the striker cannot be out LBW under any circumstances.
36.1.3 The ball, without having previously touched the bat or the hand holding the bat, first strikes the striker's person (clothing, pad, or body).
36.1.4 Point of impact:
36.1.4.1 If the striker made a genuine attempt to play the ball with the bat, the point of first impact must be in line between wicket and wicket (in-line).
36.1.4.2 If the striker offered no shot (made no genuine attempt to play the ball with the bat), the point of impact may be either in line between wicket and wicket OR outside the off stump.
36.1.5 The ball would have continued on to hit the striker's wicket (stumps).

MCC Law 36.2 - Impact outside the off stump summary
If the ball pitches outside off stump or in line, strikes the pad outside off stump, but the batter made a genuine attempt to play a stroke, the batter is NOT OUT. If the batter did not offer a shot (shouldered arms), the batter CAN be given out LBW even if struck outside the off stump, provided the ball would have hit the stumps.

================================================================================
LAW 37: OBSTRUCTING THE FIELD
================================================================================
MCC Law 37.1 - Out Obstructing the field
37.1.1 Either batter is out Obstructing the field if, except in the circumstances of 37.2, and while the ball is in play, he/she wilfully attempts to obstruct or distract the fielding side by word or action.
37.1.2 A batter is not out Obstructing the field if the obstruction or distraction is accidental, or to avoid injury.

MCC Law 37.2 - Preventing a catch
If the striker in playing a stroke deliberately uses the bat or his/her person to prevent a catch being made, the striker is out Obstructing the field.

MCC Law 37.4 - Returning the ball to a fielder
Either batter is out Obstructing the field if, without the consent of the fielding side, he/she uses the bat or person to return the ball to any fielder while the ball is still in play.

================================================================================
LAW 38: RUN OUT
================================================================================
MCC Law 38.1 - Out Run out
Either batter is out Run out if, at any time while the ball is in play, he/she is out of his/her ground and his/her wicket is fairly put down by the action of a fielder, unless Law 38.2 applies or the dismissal is a Stumped dismissal.

MCC Law 38.2 - Batter not out Run out
38.2.1 A batter is not out Run out if he/she has been within his/her ground and has subsequently left it to avoid injury.
38.2.2 If a batter in running makes good his/her ground by grounding the bat or any part of his/her body behind the popping crease, and subsequent bouncing or loss of contact occurs while the wicket is being put down, the batter is NOT out Run out (Law 38.2.1.2 - sliding bat / unbroken momentum).

MCC Law 38.3 - Non-striker leaving their ground early (Running out the non-striker)
If the non-striker is out of his/her ground at any time from the moment the ball comes into play until the instant when the bowler would normally have been expected to release the ball, the bowler is permitted to attempt to run out the non-striker. Whether the attempt is successful or not, the ball shall not count as one of the over.

================================================================================
LAW 39: STUMPED
================================================================================
MCC Law 39.1 - Out Stumped
39.1.1 The striker is out Stumped if:
39.1.1.1 a ball delivered by the bowler is not a No ball, and
39.1.1.2 the striker is out of his/her ground, and
39.1.1.3 the striker is not attempting a run, and
39.1.1.4 the wicket is fairly put down by the wicket-keeper without the intervention of another fielder.
39.1.2 If the striker is attempting a run, the mode of dismissal is Run out rather than Stumped.
39.1.3 The striker can be out Stumped off a Wide delivery.

ICC PLAYING CONDITIONS: SUPER OVER PROCEDURE
================================================================================
ICC Appendix - Super Over Procedure for Tied Matches
1. In limited-overs matches where a winner must be determined (such as knockout matches or tournament playoffs), a tied match is decided by a Super Over.
2. The team batting second in the main match bats first in the Super Over.
3. Each team bats for one over of 6 legal deliveries bowled by one selected bowler.
4. Each team may lose up to 2 wickets; the loss of the second wicket ends the team's Super Over innings immediately.
5. The team with the greater number of runs scored in their Super Over is the winner.
6. If the Super Over itself results in a tie, subsequent Super Overs shall be played until there is a winner. The boundary count-back rule was abolished by the ICC in October 2019.

================================================================================
ICC PLAYING CONDITIONS: DECISION REVIEW SYSTEM (DRS) & UMPIRE'S CALL
================================================================================
ICC Appendix - Decision Review System (DRS)
1. Player Review Requests:
   - A player wishing to review an on-field decision must signal a 'T' with both arms within 15 seconds of the dismissal or decision.
   - Only the dismissed batter or the fielding captain may request a player review.
2. Number of Reviews:
   - Each team is typically allotted 2 unsuccessful reviews per innings in Test matches and ODIs, and 2 in T20 Internationals (or as specified by tournament regulations).
   - If a review results in an "Umpire's Call" on any element (pitching, impact, or hitting the wickets), the on-field decision stands, but the reviewing team does NOT lose its review.
3. Ball Tracking & Umpire's Call in LBW Reviews:
   - Pitching: To overturn a 'Not Out' call, more than 50% of the ball must pitch within the line between wicket and wicket.
   - Impact: If the on-field decision was Not Out, more than 50% of the ball must be in-line to overturn; if the center of the ball is within the outer edges of the stumps, it is ruled In-line. If between the center and outer edge, it is Umpire's Call.
   - Wicket Hitting: For the ball to be deemed 'Hitting', some part of the ball must be projected to hit the stumps within the zone defined by the outside of the off and leg stumps and below the top of the bails. If more than 50% of the ball hits the wicket zone, it is 'Hitting'. If less than 50% of the ball is hitting the zone, it remains 'Umpire's Call'.

LAW 19: BOUNDARIES
================================================================================
MCC Law 19.1 - Determining the boundary of the field of play
Before the toss, the umpires shall agree the boundary of the field of play with both captains. The boundary shall be agreed such that no part of any sight-screen, boundary cushion, rope, or fence is outside it.

MCC Law 19.4 - Ball grounded beyond the boundary
The ball shall be treated as having crossed the boundary or being grounded beyond the boundary if it touches the boundary rope, fence, or cushion, or if it touches any object or person on or outside the boundary.

MCC Law 19.5 - Fielder grounded beyond the boundary & Airborne Relay Catch
19.5.1 A fielder is grounded beyond the boundary if any part of their person is touching the boundary rope, cushion, or the ground beyond the boundary.
19.5.2 A fielder who is not grounded beyond the boundary may field the ball, even if the ball is over the boundary line in the air.
19.5.3 For an airborne boundary relay catch: A fielder who jumps from within the field of play and parries or catches the ball while airborne is making a legal catch. If the fielder's first contact with the ball was while airborne after having last touched the ground beyond the boundary, it is deemed a boundary, NOT a fair catch. A fielder who steps outside the boundary must re-establish contact with the ground INSIDE the boundary before their first touch on the ball.

LAW 22: WIDE BALL
================================================================================
MCC Law 22.1 - Judging a Wide
22.1.1 If the bowler delivers a ball, not being a No ball, the umpire shall adjudge it a Wide if, according to the definition in 22.1.2, the ball passes wide of where the striker is standing or has stood at the crease, and which also would have passed wide of the striker standing in a normal guard position.
22.1.2 The ball will be considered as passing wide of the striker if it passes out of reach, so that the striker cannot hit it with the bat by means of a normal cricket stroke.

MCC Law 22.2 - Call and signal of Wide ball
If the umpire adjudges a delivery to be a Wide, he/she shall call and signal Wide ball as soon as the ball passes the striker's wicket. One penalty run is awarded to the batting side, in addition to any runs scored off the delivery.

MCC Law 22.4 - Delivery not a Wide
The umpire shall not adjudge a ball as being a Wide if the striker, by moving, brings the ball within reach, or if the ball touches the bat or person of the striker before passing the wicket. In limited-overs cricket, any delivery passing down the leg side behind the batter's legs is judged a Wide unless the batter switched stance.

LAW 34: HIT THE BALL TWICE
================================================================================
MCC Law 34.1 - Out Hit the ball twice
34.1.1 The striker is out Hit the ball twice if, while the ball is in play, it strikes any part of their person or is struck by their bat and, before the ball has been touched by a fielder, the striker wilfully strikes it again with the bat or person other than a hand holding the bat, except solely in defence of their wicket.
34.1.2 For the second strike to be lawful, it must be solely for the purpose of guarding the striker's wicket and must not be an attempt to score runs or prevent a catch.

MCC Law 34.2 - Lawful defence of wicket
A striker may lawfully hit the ball a second time with the bat, or with any part of their person other than a hand not holding the bat, solely to guard their wicket. However, if in making such a defensive second strike the batter attempts to score a run, or if the ball goes on to the boundary, no runs are scored and the ball becomes dead immediately upon the run attempt.

MCC Law 34.3 - Returning the ball to a fielder
Except with the consent of the fielding side, neither batter is permitted to strike or kick the ball to return it to any fielder. If done wilfully without consent, the batter is out Obstructing the field under Law 37.4.

================================================================================
LAW 40: TIMED OUT
================================================================================
MCC Law 40.1 - Out Timed out
40.1.1 After the fall of a wicket or the retirement of a batter, the incoming batter must, unless Time has been called, be in position to take guard or for the other batter to be ready to receive the next ball within 3 minutes of the dismissal or retirement. Under ICC Men's Cricket World Cup and T20 International Playing Conditions, this time limit is strictly reduced to 2 minutes.
40.1.2 If the incoming batter does not arrive at the wicket and prepare to receive the ball within the prescribed time limit (2 minutes under ICC conditions / 3 minutes under MCC Laws), the incoming batter will be given out, Timed out, upon appeal by the fielding captain.
40.1.3 Equipment delays, such as a broken helmet strap (as occurred in the Angelo Mathews dismissal in the 2023 ICC World Cup), do not pause the timer unless the batter consults the umpires before the time expires and the umpires explicitly grant time allowance.
40.1.4 An appeal from the fielding team is strictly mandatory before the umpire can declare a batter Timed out under Law 31.1.
